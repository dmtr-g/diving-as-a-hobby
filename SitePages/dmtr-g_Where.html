6 Best Dive Locations

Blue Corner, Palau: A vibrant coral wall teeming with marine life. When strong currents sweep through, vast schools of fish gather — divers use reef hooks to watch tuna, sharks, and eagle rays parade by. One of the highest shark densities of any single dive site on the planet.

Manta Night Dive, Kona, Hawaii: An illuminated underwater stage off the coast draws plankton, which in turn draws manta rays. Divers descend to sandy vantage points and watch these gentle giants glide and twirl overhead in a breathtaking nocturnal display.

Cenote Angelita, Yucatan, Mexico: At about 30 m depth, a hydrogen sulphate layer creates a visible separation between crystal-clear freshwater above and saltwater below — a cloud-like phenomenon that looks like an underwater river and makes for stunning photography.

Silfra Fissure, Iceland: Dive between the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates with visibility up to 100 m. Drift through vivid blue and green aquatic grasses and touch both continents at the same time — a truly surreal experience open year round.

Cape Kri, Raja Ampat, Indonesia: Researchers recorded a world-record number of fish species in a single dive here. Warm water, thriving coral, and a kaleidoscope of marine life make this suitable for divers of all skill levels. Just drift and take it all in.

Secret Bay (Mainit Muck), Philippines: A macro photographer's paradise in only 3–21 m of water. Look for mantis shrimp, nudibranchs, gobies, and the wonderpus octopus by day. After dark it transforms — Ambon scorpionfish, seahorses, frogfish, and Bobbit worms come out.
